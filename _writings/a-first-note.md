---
title: What is Discontentment For?
date: 2026-11-27
---

I have a very active imagination for what life could offer. I’m curious about the possibilities. 

How different would things be if I changed some of the variables:

-What if I moved to that city? 

-What would that job be like? 

-What is life like in other countries? 

However, I’m plagued by the thought that I’ll never actually discover the answers. I’ll just be left to run the mental reel once again. For years, fear of the unknown and holding on to comfort has left me to imagine. Stuck in a world of what-ifs. 

But this doesn’t stop the dreaming. It recurs without welcome and reminds me that there’s something to be experienced. Something, somewhere out there. I hope that it’s good, but I’d also like to know if it’s bad. Someone telling me a lifestyle doesn’t work or that a place isn’t as it seems isn’t good enough. I crave the experience of discovering it for myself and until then I’m left with all these blanks sections on my mental map. Maybe you’re like me. You have a comfortable job that pays your bills, but you don’t feel like you’re in the right place. It’s hard to find meaningful connections, whether in friendship or in romance. You feel like you have to sleuth day in and day out just to find an event you’d feel excited about attending. You’re tired of trying to make things happen and coming back empty handed. You just want to feel the energy of life. Like things are happening and you’re apart of them. This is at least how I feel. Maybe there’s a place where all of this happens. Where the people I’ll encounter are aligned to my values, where a community forms around something that I can’t help but be excited about. I’d like to find this place, but it’s all so scary. A myriad of excuses reveal themselves. The expenses, the possibility of failure, the unknown. That maybe I’ll end up in a place worse than I started.

**The context, as briefly as possible.**

I live in a one-bedroom apartment in the Dilworth neighborhood of Charlotte, NC, working for a large bank in the city. Although I’m now in the center of the metropolitan area, I grew up in the suburbs for most of my life. Other than a handful of faded memories of Tim Horton’s and slush encrusted winter boots from my childhood up north, this city is all that I knew growing up. My family moved frequently, so I became intimately familiar with all that Charlotte has to offer. I’ve called home a total of three counties and experienced the breadth of housing arrangements. A townhouse in semi-rural Monroe, a cockroach infested duplex in a not-so-affluent area off South Boulevard, an apartment in the upscale south Charlotte neighborhood of Ballantyne, and a single family home in northeastern town of Harrisburg. I went to UNC Charlotte for my first year of college, the city’s biggest university and that’s when it hit me. I needed something new. I was in Charlotte for too long, my life wouldn’t go anywhere if I stuck around. The trajectory I was on wasn’t going to work for me and I needed it to change. I had begun to dream of studying abroad in France, but I didn’t know how to make it happen. I ended up picking a realistic and safe option, finishing my remaining two and a half years of college in Virginia. But once I graduated, I moved back.

**Why did I move back and why do I want to leave?**

There are two reasons I moved back after college:

1. I hadn’t received a full-time offer yet, so with a diploma and negative money (thanks student loans), I thought I’d job search at home.

2. I received a degree in finance, an industry where Charlotte abounds with opportunity.

After four months of full-time job searching I got my first offer and began my career in finance. It consisted of a lot of data analysis, revenue projections, mergers & acquisitions, and plenty of other buzz words ripe for resume inclusion. Working at this job was when I first felt in the totality of my being that I was fundamentally and categorically misplaced. It was a toxic work environment, but even as I switched to my current job I realized that that had been only part of the issue. There was still a spiritual misalignment in the work itself. Prior to graduation I was in the pipeline. I was stoked to potentially work in investments or maybe even be an entrepreneur. I thought making money would be exciting and business would be fulfilling. 

It wasn’t. 

I have learned so much about myself through this experience and I realized that this superficial interest was apart of the chaff I needed to winnow to find out who I really am. I won’t enumerate all of my discontentment with the corporate world, there are plenty of people who have already excellently identified the issues, but I’ll give a couple high-level. I prioritize autonomy and working somewhere that dictates where you have to be and how long has never sat right with me. When I would walk into the office with beautiful weather and the sun shining on face, I would wish I didn’t have to be inside for the next eight hours. Additionally, I was never able to feel like what I did was interesting, or that it truly mattered. Ultimately though, there’s a certain ineffable nature to how we feel deep down. We try our bests to explain, but all we know is that something off. Even if we think we know why, we may not discover the full picture until much later. You may resonate with this even if your reasons are entirely different. Charlotte in my experience has been a hub for transplants all across the country who have come for one reason: a corporate job. Most of the people I interact with are finance or tech professionals who seem perfectly content in their roles. They see themselves moving up or getting masters degrees. Rewarding themselves with what the lifestyle has to offer. Anytime you’re misaligned it’s not a fault of either party involved. It’s just that you’re values or priorities are different. Charlotte has a reputation for being boring or having little culture. While every place has culture (you’re assuming America is a default when you say that is has no culture, that another place is more “exotic” or “cultural”), when you’ve been in the same one for a while it can begin to feel that way. This isn’t going to ring true for every person. Sometimes being in the same place for 30 years cements it as home, as comforting. I personally have found that it’s been more effort than it should be to feel at home here.

Discontentment is often very difficult to explicate. It’s a subjective feeling that many people just won’t connect with. It can even be hard to understand yourself. Why can’t I just be happy where I am? Why do I hate my job when a lot of other people would feel incredibly grateful for it? If I really wanted to I could blame my childhood for this. Repeatedly watching my dad’s DVD copy of Planet Earth had me fascinated by diversity of our planet, or maybe it was the Hong Kong martial arts movies which imbued in me a curiosity for different cultures. Whatever the reason for my discontent, I’ve learned that something which persists for years is not something you can shove back down. 

Discontentment persists inside you to say, “hey, you’re in the wrong place”. Whether that be a career, friend group, location. It’s like the game hotter or colder. You don’t know what you’re looking for but you’re given clues whether you’re getting closer or farther. This is what discontentment is for. It’s a cue to try something new so you can get started (or continue) in the direction you need to go. 

**Where do I want to go?**

Recently I’ve been reading Taoist writings and in Taoism there’s a concept called Wu-Wei. It’s often translated as non-action or effortless action. It’s this idea that when you’re aligned to the Way then things should come naturally. Not that things won’t be hard, or require effort, but that it should feel natural. Even in Taoism they don’t explain what the Way is, it’s ineffable. Similarly with finding your place. You don’t know ahead of time who your people are, or where you should be, or what you should do. It should however feel natural. It should excite you to work through the long nights, or allow you to wake up thankful for another day. At least this is how I understand it. I want to feel the energy that comes with being where I need to be. A quote attributed to Confucius says:

>“Don’t make the Way too complicated. If it’s complicated, there’s too much to do; if there’s too much to do, it’s confusing; if it’s confusing you get anxious; if you get too anxious, you can’t save yourself”. 

If you’re in a situation where everything feels like heavy lifting, maybe that’s a sign you’re in the wrong place. Some things certainly will remain difficult, but nonetheless they should feel right. Like it’s hard for a reason. I want to live a life where I’m fighting for something, not fighting against it.

So what am I going to do? Long term I don’t know, but in the mean time I’m going to take a backpack and explore the world with the savings I’ve build up over the past three years of a corporate career. The decision is different for everyone, and there’s no right answer, but I believe that overthinking can cause life to pass you by through inaction. I don’t come from a wealthy background, and though I have a loving family with a spare bed, there are risks. Call it a sabbatical. I don’t know what I will learn about myself, I don’t know how it will help, but a step taken in conviction will never be a wrong step. I’ve always wondered what was out there. I’m 25 with no kids, no wife, the ability to start fresh anywhere in the world. I can’t say with confidence that it will work out, or that I will find anything, but I can always course correct. Eventually when it’s all said in done I will be able to say that I tried and I didn’t let fear guide my steps.

I’m confident there is somewhere for you and I. We just need to find it.
